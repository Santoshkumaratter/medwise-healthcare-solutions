AAPC credential

CPC — Certified Professional Coder

The credential named in most fresher medical coding job postings in India. If you are choosing one certification to start a career with, this is almost always it.

CPC covers the physician and outpatient side of coding: the work done for doctors' offices, ambulatory surgery centres and hospital outpatient departments. You learn to read a clinical note and assign the diagnosis codes that justify the treatment, the procedure codes that describe it, and the modifiers that explain the circumstances.

It is an open-book exam, which sounds forgiving and is not. Everyone in the hall has the same three manuals. The candidates who pass are the ones who can find a code in fifteen seconds instead of two minutes, and we spend real classroom time on exactly that skill.

Syllabus

  • Medical terminology, anatomy and physiology, taught for coding rather than for exams
  • ICD-10-CM structure, conventions and official guidelines, chapter by chapter
  • CPT: surgery sections, radiology, pathology and laboratory, medicine, anaesthesia
  • HCPCS Level II — drugs, injections, supplies and durable medical equipment
  • Modifiers, bundling logic, NCCI edits and correct code sequencing
  • Evaluation and Management levelling under the current guidelines
  • Compliance, HIPAA, medical necessity and documentation improvement
  • Revenue cycle context — what happens to a claim after you code it
  • Manual tabbing workshop and the three-pass exam strategy
  • Full-length timed mock exams with question-by-question review

AHIMA credential

CCS — Certified Coding Specialist

The hospital-side credential. Harder, narrower, and generally attached to better-paying roles than entry-level outpatient coding.

CCS is built around inpatient facility coding, where a single admission can involve a dozen diagnoses, several procedures and a principal diagnosis decision that changes what the hospital is paid. That decision is what MS-DRG assignment turns on, and it is why experienced inpatient coders are valued the way they are.

The system you will spend most time in is ICD-10-PCS, which is a genuinely different discipline from CPT — seven characters built from root operations, body parts, approaches and devices rather than looked up from an index. It takes longer to learn, and far fewer coders in India have it.

Syllabus

  • Advanced anatomy, physiology and disease processes for inpatient records
  • ICD-10-CM applied to inpatient admissions, including comorbidities and complications
  • ICD-10-PCS in depth — root operations, body part values, approaches and devices
  • Principal diagnosis selection and the UHDDS definitions that govern it
  • MS-DRG assignment and the effect of CC and MCC capture on reimbursement
  • Present on Admission indicators and hospital-acquired conditions
  • Hospital outpatient and ambulatory surgery coding with CPT
  • Case-based practice on full inpatient records rather than short scenarios
  • Mock exams matched to the AHIMA format, including scenario questions
